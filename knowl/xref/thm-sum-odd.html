Theorem 3.1.4 Sum of Odd Numbers.

For every natural number \(n\text{,}\) the sum of the first \(n\) odd numbers is \(n^2\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗

Proof.
We use induction on \(n\text{.}\) For \(n = 1\) the sum is \(1 = 1^2\text{.}\) Suppose the claim holds for \(n\text{.}\) Then
🔗
\begin{equation*} 1 + 3 + \cdots + (2n-1) + (2n+1) = n^2 + 2n + 1 = (n+1)^2\text{,} \end{equation*}
so the claim holds for \(n+1\text{.}\)
